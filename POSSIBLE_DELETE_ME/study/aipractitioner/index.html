DOMAIN 1

Fundamentals of AI and ML

20% weight · ~10 scored questions

Domain 1 builds the vocabulary you'll use everywhere else: what AI, machine learning, and deep learning actually mean, and how they relate to each other. You'll learn to tell supervised learning apart from unsupervised and reinforcement learning, and to match common business problems with the right technique. It also walks through the AI/ML development lifecycle, from collecting data to monitoring a model after launch.

Supervised vs. unsupervised vs. reinforcement learning AI/ML development lifecycle Types of data & inferencing Model performance metrics AWS managed AI services
Amazon SageMaker AI Amazon Comprehend Amazon Rekognition
Exam watch-out Amazon Polly and Amazon Transcribe are opposites, and the exam loves testing this. Polly turns text into speech; Transcribe turns speech into text. If a question describes converting a call recording into a written transcript, that's Transcribe, not Polly.

DOMAIN 2

Fundamentals of Generative AI

24% weight · ~12 scored questions

Domain 2 is your introduction to generative AI: the technology behind chatbots, writing assistants, and image generators. You'll learn the building blocks — tokens, embeddings, and context windows — along with how agentic AI lets a model take multi-step actions on its own. This domain also covers Amazon Bedrock, the AWS platform most other domains build on.

Tokens & embeddings Context windows & context engineering Foundation model lifecycle Agentic AI & MCP GenAI advantages vs. limitations
Amazon Bedrock Amazon Q Amazon Bedrock AgentCore
Exam watch-out Strands Agents and Amazon Bedrock AgentCore are not the same layer. Strands Agents is the open-source framework you use to build an agent; AgentCore is the managed AWS service that runs, secures, and scales that agent in production.
Not Started
DOMAIN 3

Applications of Foundation Models

28% weight · ~14 scored questions

Domain 3 is the largest and most applied domain on the exam: making a foundation model actually work for a real business problem. You'll practice choosing the right model, writing effective prompts, and deciding between fine-tuning and Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG) to ground answers in company data. It also covers how to measure whether a model is genuinely doing its job well.

Prompt engineering techniques Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG) FM customization tradeoffs Fine-tuning & RLHF Model evaluation metrics
Bedrock Knowledge Bases Amazon OpenSearch Service Bedrock Model Evaluation
Exam watch-out RAG and fine-tuning solve different problems. RAG retrieves outside information at the moment of the request without changing the model itself; fine-tuning actually retrains the model's weights on new examples. If a question is about grounding answers in current company documents, think RAG first.
Not Started
DOMAIN 4

Guidelines for Responsible AI

14% weight · ~7 scored questions

Domain 4 is about the human side of AI: fairness, bias, transparency, and explainability. You'll learn what makes a training dataset responsible, what legal risks come with generative AI, and which AWS tools help detect and document bias. The goal is recognizing the guardrails that keep AI systems trustworthy, not building them from scratch.

Bias & fairness Transparency & explainability Responsible dataset characteristics Legal & IP risk Environmental sustainability
SageMaker Clarify SageMaker Model Cards Amazon A2I
Exam watch-out SageMaker Clarify, Amazon A2I, and SageMaker Model Monitor get mixed up constantly. Clarify detects bias and explains predictions; A2I routes a low-confidence prediction to a human reviewer; Model Monitor watches a deployed model's performance over time.
Not Started
DOMAIN 5

Security, Compliance & Governance

14% weight · ~7 scored questions

Domain 5 applies security and compliance thinking to AI systems specifically. You'll learn AI-specific threats like prompt injection and data leakage, how the AWS Shared Responsibility Model applies to services like Bedrock and SageMaker, and which AWS services support governance and audits. It's general AWS security knowledge, refocused on the unique risks of AI workloads.

AWS Shared Responsibility Model Prompt injection & data leakage Governance & audit tools Data governance strategies Hallucination grounding techniques
AWS IAM AWS CloudTrail AWS Artifact
Exam watch-out AWS Artifact and AWS Audit Manager sound interchangeable but aren't. Artifact is where you download AWS's own compliance reports; Audit Manager is what you use to collect evidence of your own organization's compliance.

Domain weights

  • D1 Fundamentals of AI and ML — 20%
  • D2 Fundamentals of GenAI — 24%
  • D3 Applications of Foundation Models — 28%
  • D4 Guidelines for Responsible AI — 14%
  • D5 Security, Compliance & Governance — 14%

Service comparisons

  • Bedrock vs. SageMaker AI: Bedrock = pre-built FM access | SageMaker AI = build your own
  • Macie vs. Inspector: Macie = finds sensitive data in S3 | Inspector = scans compute for vulnerabilities
  • CloudTrail vs. CloudWatch: CloudTrail = logs who did what | CloudWatch = monitors performance and health
  • Artifact vs. Audit Manager: Artifact = download AWS's reports | Audit Manager = build your own evidence
  • Polly vs. Transcribe: Polly = text to speech | Transcribe = speech to text

Exam clue phrases

  • "without managing servers": points to a managed or serverless AWS service
  • "ground responses in your own data": points to RAG / Bedrock Knowledge Bases
  • "same prompt, different answer": points to nondeterminism
  • "filter content before it reaches the user": points to Bedrock Guardrails
  • "who is responsible for what": points to the Shared Responsibility Model

Responsible AI principles

  • Fairness — equal, just treatment of all groups
  • Inclusivity — works well for diverse populations
  • Robustness — reliable under varied conditions
  • Safety — avoids harmful outputs or actions
  • Veracity — truthful, accurate outputs
  • Transparency — people understand how outputs are produced
  • Explainability — decisions can be interpreted and justified
