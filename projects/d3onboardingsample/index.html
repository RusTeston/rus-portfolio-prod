Section 1 Executive Summary
What This Document Is and Why It Was Created

This document is the D3 Phase 1 New Hire Onboarding Framework, a corporate-ready, operationally complete onboarding program for every person who will enter the D3 Dallas data center during Phase 1: Build-Out Mobilization and Site Readiness. It was created to ensure that D3's first wave of personnel, drawn from at least seven distinct organizations including D3 direct staff, a general contractor, multiple MEP subcontractors, a low-voltage/cabling contractor, a commissioning agent, a logistics vendor, and an early operations team, arrive with a consistent baseline of safety knowledge, site access credentials, role-specific technical readiness, and documentation competency before performing any work.

Data center build-outs for hyperscale and AI infrastructure clients operate on compressed schedules with near-zero tolerance for rework, safety incidents, or documentation failures. The gap between a well-onboarded workforce and a poorly onboarded one is measured not in training hours but in schedule weeks, safety events, and client confidence. This framework closes that gap.

Section 2 Data Center Build-Out Lifecycle

A data center build-out begins when the building shell contractor substantially completes and turns over the structure, typically meaning a watertight envelope, base-building MEP rough-ins to a defined scope boundary, and a certificate of occupancy or temporary certificate for the shell. From that point, a specialized build-out contractor or delivery organization executes seven sequential but partially overlapping phases before the end customer takes operational possession.

Full Build-Out Lifecycle, Phase 0 Through Phase 7

Phase 0 Building Shell & Base Building Completion Civil, structure, envelope, base-building MEP to defined scope boundary, life-safety systems rough-in, site utilities at building entry points. 12–24 months Substantial completion certificate; AHJ inspection signed; utility interconnect agreements executed; site control transferred to build-out contractor.
Phase 1 Build-Out Mobilization and Site Readiness Establish site control, EHS program, logistics infrastructure, project controls, QA/QC processes, commissioning planning, and design-readiness for MEP rough-in. Phase 3 White-Space Fit-Out & ICT Infrastructure Raised floor (if applicable), cable containment, ladder rack, overhead raceways, hot/cold aisle containment, structured cabling horizontal and vertical distribution, fiber backbone, patch fields, labeling. 8–16 weeks (overlaps Phase 2 closeout) Cable pathways tested and labeled; BICSI 002 documentation package complete; ESD protocols in place; white-space access control active.

Why Phase 1 Is the Most Critical Onboarding Window

Phase 1 is not a construction phase. It is a readiness phase. Every installation, inspection, test, and commissioning activity in Phases 2 through 7 depends on decisions, documents, credentials, and site controls established in Phase 1. A Superintendent who arrives without understanding the permit-to-work system, a Commissioning Manager who doesn't receive the IFC drawing package until week six, or a Logistics Manager who sets up the laydown yard in a location that blocks equipment delivery routes, these are Phase 1 failures that cannot be recovered without schedule impact. The onboarding program exists to prevent them.

Phase 1 Principle: No construction trade begins installation work until the site safety program is activated, TDLR licenses are verified, the commissioning plan is at Level 2, and all Tier 1 roles have cleared the Day 1 Readiness Gate. This is a hard gate, not a guideline. D3
Section 3 Phase 1 Definition and Scope
Formal Phase 1 Definition

Phase 1: Build-Out Mobilization and Site Readiness formally begins when the building shell contractor executes a written site turnover to D3 or the build-out contractor, supported by a punch-list-cleared substantial completion certificate, an executed AHJ inspection record for the shell scope, and confirmation that base-building utility interconnects are in place. It ends when all Phase 1 exit criteria are satisfied and the site is formally authorized to begin MEP rough-in.

Contractor vs. D3 Employee Distinction

This program operates on a two-track onboarding design: D3 direct employees complete the full program through D3 HR and receive D3 policies, benefits, and performance management from day one. General contractor and subcontractor personnel complete the universal curriculum and their role-specific track through a joint delivery model, D3 EHS delivers the universal safety and site-specific modules; the GC or subcontractor delivers their organization's own HR and employment modules and is responsible for their own TDLR license verification and compliance records. D3 retains records of all safety and site-access training regardless of employer. D3

  • Documentation quality score reviewed: error rate target below 5% on reviewed work products

  • Competency demonstration: role-specific field exercise or document review scenario
Days 61–90 Full independent productivity
  • Full independent task execution with periodic supervisor check-in (not sign-off on every item)
  • Stretch goals assigned where applicable: lead a JHA walkthrough, train an incoming Phase 1 or Phase 2 employee, present at a coordination meeting
  • Mentorship assignment for next-wave hires where this person is qualified to orient others
  • Recommended certifications pursued where applicable (OSHA 30, BICSI TECH, RCDD, CSP, etc.) BP

  • Formal performance review: supervisor + HR (D3 direct employees) or supervisor + GC lead (contractor personnel)
  • LMS completion report reviewed: all required modules confirmed complete
  • Safety and documentation metrics reviewed against six onboarding KPIs (see Section 16)
  • Transition to standard performance management cycle; onboarding program formally closed
  • Phase 2 readiness flag set: is this person ready to support Phase 2 activities, or does additional development apply?

High High U-14 (Documentation Workflow) is an R-30 module with a practical PMIS exercise. Day 30 Gate includes review of 3 work products for documentation quality. QA/QC Manager flags NCRs for recurring documentation errors.
R-05 ERCOT permanent power timing not confirmed, causing Phase 4 delay, electrical commissioning cannot proceed without utility energization, and ERCOT interconnection requests for large loads (75 MW+) are subject to extended review under Texas SB 6. Medium High ERCOT energization gate is a Phase 1 exit criterion. Owner's PM and Commissioning Manager are briefed on ERCOT SB 6 large-load requirements in Track T1 and Track T3. Confirmed power date is locked into the schedule baseline before Phase 2 begins.
R-06 Texas heat illness event during Phase 1 mobilization, Dallas summer temperatures regularly exceed 100°F; outdoor and partially conditioned spaces during mobilization create significant risk of heat exhaustion or heat stroke, especially for new arrivals. High (May–Sep) High U-13 (Texas Heat Illness Prevention) is required for all outdoor and non-conditioned-space workers before accessing those areas. EHS Manager administers the Texas SB 11 protocol including temperature monitoring triggers, mandatory rest/water schedules, and emergency response procedures.
R-07 Cross-trade coordination failures caused by onboarding sequencing misalignment, a trade crew begins work before its Lead is fully oriented, creating rework, safety exposure, and schedule loss. Medium High Mobilization sequence (Section 3) mandates that Trade Leads complete Week 1 onboarding before crews arrive. The cross-role dependency matrix (Appendix A) is used to sequence onboarding so that upstream roles always precede dependent downstream roles.
R-08 ESD damage to critical IT or network hardware during white-space work, single ESD event can silently damage equipment worth tens or hundreds of thousands of dollars, often not discovered until commissioning testing. Medium High T5-02 (White-Space Cleanliness) and T5-03 (ESD Prevention) are pre-access requirements for all Group 5 personnel entering white-space areas. Supervised demonstration of ESD procedures is required before first independent white-space access.
R-09 Onboarding program not deployed before mobilization begins, D3 HR has not built the LMS course library, trained facilitators, or established the badging integration; personnel arrive with no program to go through. Medium High Section 15 (Implementation Roadmap) defines an 8-week pre-mobilization deployment plan with clear owners, deliverables, and weekly milestones. Program must be operationally ready 2 weeks before the first Wave 1 person arrives.
R-10 Readiness Gate pass rate falls below 85%, systematic failure at Day 30 indicates the universal curriculum is not effective, role-specific tracks are too thin, or the hiring profile for certain roles is misaligned with Phase 1 requirements. Low–Medium Medium Metric M-4 tracks Day 30 Gate pass rate in real time. If the first-attempt pass rate falls below 85%, D3 HR and EHS Manager convene within 5 business days to diagnose whether the gap is curriculum quality, hiring profile, or onboarding delivery, and issue a remediation action within 10 business days.

Section 17 Governance and Program Maintenance

The D3 Phase 1 Onboarding Program is owned by the D3 HR Site Lead, in partnership with the EHS Manager (safety and compliance currency) and the Site Project Manager (schedule alignment and role-group applicability). Feedback is collected through three channels and used to update the program quarterly or at phase transitions:

  • New-hire end-of-week feedback form (anonymous, 5-question, submitted at end of Week 1 and end of Week 4)
  • Supervisor Day 30 and Day 90 survey (structured, 5-point scale, role-specific, tied to Metric M-6)

Extension to Phase 2 and Beyond

This Phase 1 framework is designed as the foundation for a project-lifecycle onboarding system. At each phase transition, D3 HR reviews the active role groups, identifies new roles entering in the next phase, and builds the incremental track additions required. The universal curriculum modules (U-01 through U-18) are reused for Phase 2+ new arrivals, they do not need to be rebuilt. Role-specific tracks for MEP commissioning, equipment installation, and IST preparation are added at Phase 4 and Phase 5 transitions. By Phase 7, the Early Operations and Customer-Readiness team (Group 7) transitions from onboarding to operations-readiness training, a distinct but related program stream that should be built no later than Phase 5 mobilization.

Appendix C
Glossary of Key Terms
AHJ, Authority Having Jurisdiction
The organization, office, or individual responsible for enforcing the requirements of a code or standard, or their designated representative. For D3 Dallas, the primary AHJ for building construction is the City of Dallas Building Inspection Division.
Arc Flash
A sudden release of electrical energy through the air when a high-voltage gap exists or when high-voltage contacts are exposed. Regulated by NFPA 70E, which requires arc-flash hazard analysis and labeling on equipment likely to be energized during service.
ASHRAE Guideline 0, The Commissioning Process
An industry best-practice standard published by the American Society of Heating, Refrigerating and Air-Conditioning Engineers that defines a systematic process for the commissioning of building systems, including data center critical infrastructure.
BICSI 002
ANSI/BICSI 002: Data Center Design and Implementation Best Practices. An industry best-practice document published by BICSI (Building Industry Consulting Service International) covering design and installation of ICT infrastructure in data centers.
BMS, Building Management System
A computer-based control system installed in buildings that controls and monitors the building's mechanical and electrical equipment such as ventilation, lighting, power systems, fire systems, and security systems.
CxA: Commissioning Authority / Commissioning Agent
The individual or organization responsible for managing the commissioning process on behalf of the owner. In a data center context, the CxA typically reports directly to the owner and is independent of the contractor.
CRAH / CRAC, Computer Room Air Handler / Air Conditioner
Precision cooling units designed to maintain temperature and humidity in data center white-space environments. CRAHs use chilled-water coils; CRACs use direct-expansion refrigerant circuits.
DCIM, Data Center Infrastructure Management
Software tools and systems that manage, monitor, and analyze data center infrastructure components including power, cooling, and IT equipment. Used for asset tracking, capacity planning, and operational monitoring.
EAP, Emergency Action Plan
A written document required by OSHA 29 CFR 1926.35 that covers the actions employers and employees must take to ensure employee safety from fire and other emergencies. Must include evacuation routes, muster points, emergency contacts, and medical response procedures.
ERCOT, Electric Reliability Council of Texas
The independent system operator that manages the flow of electric power to approximately 90% of Texas's electric load. Large-load customers (75 MW+) are subject to ERCOT's large-load interconnection process; Texas SB 6 (2025) granted ERCOT emergency disconnection authority for these customers.
ESD, Electrostatic Discharge
A sudden flow of electricity between two electrically charged objects caused by contact, an electrical short, or dielectric breakdown. A significant hazard in data center white-space environments where sensitive electronic components are handled.
IFC, Issued for Construction
A drawing or document that has been reviewed, approved, and formally released to trade contractors as the authoritative basis for construction. No installation should begin from drawings that have not reached IFC status.
IST: Integrated Systems Testing
A commissioning phase (Phase 6 in this framework) in which all major facility systems are tested together to verify their integrated performance, including failover, redundancy, and emergency scenario responses. Equivalent to Uptime Institute Level 5 commissioning.
ITP, Inspection and Test Plan
A document that defines the required inspections, tests, and witness points for a scope of work, identifies hold points requiring third-party or owner approval before work can continue, and specifies the responsible party for each check.
JHA, Job Hazard Analysis
Also called Job Safety Analysis (JSA). A technique that focuses on job tasks to identify hazards before they occur, used to train workers in safe work methods and as a pre-task planning tool. Required by OSHA 29 CFR 1926.21.
LMS, Learning Management System
A software platform used to administer, track, report, and deliver training programs. Examples include Workday Learning, SAP SuccessFactors, and Cornerstone OnDemand. This framework is designed for integration with any standard LMS platform.
LOTO, Lockout/Tagout
An energy control procedure regulated by OSHA 29 CFR 1910.147 (general industry) and OSHA 29 CFR 1926.417 (construction) that requires authorized workers to de-energize and physically lock out equipment before performing maintenance or service to prevent accidental re-energization.
MEP, Mechanical, Electrical, and Plumbing
The three major building systems that form the functional infrastructure of a data center: mechanical (HVAC, cooling), electrical (power distribution, UPS, generators), and plumbing (domestic water, fire suppression, condensate).
MOP / SOP / EOP
Method of Procedure / Standard Operating Procedure / Emergency Operating Procedure. Formal documented instructions for performing routine, standard, and emergency operational tasks in a data center. Required as turnover deliverables before customer handoff.
MVT, Minimum Viable Team
The minimum set of roles that must be fully onboarded and operational before general worker access to the D3 site can begin. Defined in this framework as nine specific roles across Groups 1, 2, 3, and 6.
NCR, Non-Conformance Report
A quality documentation instrument used to record any installation, material, or process that does not conform to the approved design, specification, or inspection standard. NCRs require corrective action documentation and closure before the associated work can be signed off for commissioning.
PMIS, Project Management Information System
A software platform used on construction projects to manage drawings, RFIs, submittals, change orders, daily reports, and other project documentation. Examples include Procore, Oracle Primavera Unifier, and Autodesk Construction Cloud.
PTW, Permit-to-Work
A formal written system used to control potentially hazardous work. A PTW authorizes specific people to carry out specific work within a defined time frame while setting out the precautions required to complete the work safely. Required on energized, confined-space, hot-work, and other high-hazard scopes.
RACI
A responsibility assignment matrix that identifies who is Responsible (does the work), Accountable (owns the outcome and answers for it), Consulted (provides input before decisions are made), and Informed (kept updated on decisions and actions).
RCDD, Registered Communications Distribution Designer
A BICSI credential for ICT infrastructure designers. Requires two years of verifiable ICT design experience with a current BICSI credential or two years of higher education, or five years of verifiable ICT experience. Many hyperscale data center clients mandate RCDD involvement in cabling system design.
SIMOPS, Simultaneous Operations
The condition in which two or more different work activities are being conducted in the same area at the same time, each potentially creating hazards for the other. SIMOPS management is a critical function of the PTW Coordinator on a multi-trade data center build-out.
TDLR, Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation
The Texas state agency that licenses and regulates a wide range of occupations and industries, including electricians (Chapter 1305), HVAC/refrigeration contractors (Chapter 1302), and alarm system installers (Chapter 1702).
Uptime Institute Tier Standard
A globally recognized certification program that rates data center infrastructure on a four-tier scale (Tier I through Tier IV) based on redundancy, fault tolerance, and concurrent maintainability. The Uptime Institute also publishes a 5-level commissioning framework referenced in this document.
WBS, Work Breakdown Structure
A hierarchical decomposition of the total scope of work to be carried out by the project team to accomplish the project objectives and create the required deliverables. The WBS is the foundation of the project schedule and cost baseline.
